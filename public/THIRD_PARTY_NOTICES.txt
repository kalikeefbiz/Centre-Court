# Locomotion references and attribution

PlayCanvas engine `scripts/esm/third-person-controller.mjs`, examined at
209ce5752527ab5050ec9632ca6dc1272542b70d:
https://github.com/playcanvas/engine/blob/209ce5752527ab5050ec9632ca6dc1272542b70d/scripts/esm/third-person-controller.mjs

`damp` and `_updateController` informed the time-based exponential velocity feedback,
horizontal speed and travel direction. Centre Court expresses the damping as a
per-second rate, converges on a desired velocity with a bounded acceleration,
and uses its existing AI vector-delta limiter. Input uses the upstream normalized
direction technique while preserving analog length. No rigidbody, camera or jump code.

OpenTennis `crates/hst-sim/src/player.rs`, `crates/hst-sim/src/motion.rs`, and
`research/journal/2026-10-07-p7-movement/README.md`, examined at
50585580ef2d5748d2b44ee37184c9520bf299b2:
https://github.com/FIZL0/opentennis/tree/50585580ef2d5748d2b44ee37184c9520bf299b2

Examined `run_speed`, `run_velocity`, `angle`, `run_motion`, `Body::step/go/toward`,
`recovery`, `follow_over`, `serve_recovery`, and `serve_over`. Used only as a
behavioral/state-organization reference: shared human/AI running, separate physical
travel and directional motion, agility versus pace, and explicit stroke states.
The run code immediately assigns direction and uses per-frame speeds and commercial
motion paths; those algorithms, constants, character stats and data were not ported.
Forward/lateral/backward speed differences are Centre Court tuning, not an upstream
biomechanical claim. No stamina, dash, root motion or new stroke locks were added.
