# Current limitations

- Physical iPhone Safari testing has not been performed. Desktop Chromium and Linux WebKit with mobile emulation are the automated targets; device heat, battery usage, Safari chrome/safe areas and actual multi-touch feel need hardware verification.
- Kira uses the owner’s Pink Valkyrie GLB with 13 clips and a separate racket. Several generated motions are stylized: slice/lob and volley identification carry medium confidence, as recorded in the inspection registry. Fingers remain partly open in some source poses. Fast contact seeks, lateral foot sliding and a gap on stretched contacts can remain. Arm correction is capped at 35° per joint; it never drags the gameplay position. The first rendered contact samples the recorded collision point with at most 18 cm string-bed correction, then converges onto the current simulation over 65 ms. This is not a full physical racket/cloth/finger solver.
- The ball has regulation 0.067 m visual diameter and a separate 0.0335 m collision sphere. The default full-court cameras consequently show it at only a few pixels; the existing trail and landing marker help tracking. Desktop magnification or the supplemental material preview shows its red felt, lime crown and seams. Hardware readability needs owner acceptance.
- Contact zones are configurable by stroke with a 250 ms release window. Simplified restitution remains; there is no spin aerodynamics, racket rigid-body collision, player/ball body collision, manual serve toss or foot-fault system. Serve stance remains locked legally behind the baseline.
- A below-tape net collision immediately ends the shot. Grazing top cords continue physically and can produce serve lets. Not every professional net-cord scenario is modeled.
- Presentation keeps the human at the near end. Service order and service courts change correctly; physical end changes are not animated.
- Broadcast framing prioritizes full-court visibility. Portrait play makes athletes smaller; landscape is useful for reading contacts. The behind-player mode is lower but retains an elevated arcade perspective to show the far court.
- Short set configuration is the fixed two-game preset; Standard is best of three six-game sets. There is no custom rules editor or no-ad scoring.
- No sound, replay system, detailed crowds or final stadium art. Shadows are inexpensive projected silhouettes rather than dynamic shadow maps. Prediction markers help read the first bounce.
- The runtime character/racket/ball GLBs total about 17.2 MB with nine embedded maps, loaded once without cloning. Original uploads are retained for rollback/reproduction. The bundled PlayCanvas runtime is roughly 0.53 MB gzipped plus app code and fonts. There is no offline service worker. WebGL support is required.
- Slow frames are capped at 100 ms of catch-up, so severe sustained overload slows simulation rather than allowing large physics jumps. Determinism is for identical seed/commands on this implementation, not a network lockstep guarantee across arbitrary engines or future releases.
- AI retains its original contact reach and tactics; trajectory prediction knows current ball velocity. Playable Kira uses the calibrated racket contact zones. Reaction is delayed, movement is speed limited, and seeded shot error/missed returns prevent perfect play. Difficulty and game feel will benefit from user playtesting.

- Kira, the separate racket and Royal Rally ball are required for human play. A slow/failed asset load now keeps Play and Practice disabled and offers a retry; it never silently substitutes the temporary athlete. A physical iPhone retest of the hotfix remains necessary.
